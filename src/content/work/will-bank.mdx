## Context

I joined Will Bank as a Visual Designer focused on Motion Design, paired with illustrator **Jaime Maia** — the two of us brought in specifically to raise the level of craft across the app. The illustration library had grown fast alongside the product, piece by piece, without ever stopping to ask what it should look like as a whole.

Running value-proposition dynamics with the product team, we mapped pains across the org.

## The circle

One shape holds the whole system together — Will's window to the world.

<div class="case-media">
  <InlineSvg src="/assets/will-bank/slides/circulo.svg" alt="The Circle" />
</div>

Sometimes it's a backdrop, setting the scene behind a character in the foreground; sometimes it's the protagonist itself, the frame a moment happens inside. Illustrations are built around circles and curved lines, reinforcing one of the brand's key visual elements.

<h3 class="case-subhead">Illustration Categories</h3>

Every illustration in the new system falls into one of four categories:

<div class="case-media case-media--wide">
  <img src="/assets/will-bank/categories.png" alt="The four illustration categories: scene, spot, hero, and avatar" />
</div>

- **Scene** — complex ideas translated into clear and accessible visuals
- **Spot** — a direct and literal representation of objects or actions
- **Hero** — inspirational concepts expressed through playful metaphors
- **Avatar** — visual representations of subjective traits or personalities

<h3 class="case-subhead">Skin Tone Palette</h3>

Skin tones and hairstyles were expanded well beyond the original set — and, deliberately, so was who gets illustrated: wheelchair users, people with prosthetics, older clients. Will's clients, drawn as they actually are.

## Built to last past us

A new style is easy to lose the moment the two people who made it move on. So alongside the illustrations themselves, we shipped the system behind them: a **guide for new illustrations**, a **guide for how and where to use them**, a shared **illustration bank** in Figma and Drive, and an **illustration system** built like a "doll-maker" so anyone on the team could assemble a new, on-brand character without starting from scratch. Both product and marketing signed off on the new style through structured feedback rounds before it rolled out.

Anatomy rules were part of the same system: **stylized body proportions**, exaggerated **large hands**, and **large feet** — so any new character stayed consistent, no matter who drew it.

## Motion Applications

Illustration was only half the system — motion carried it the rest of the way. Loading states, onboarding transitions, in-app celebrations: each one built from the same circle, the same characters, moving with the same "positive banking" energy the static illustrations set out to capture.
